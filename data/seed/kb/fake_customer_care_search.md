title: Fake Customer Support Numbers on Search Engines and Maps
---
Search engine manipulation and Google Maps listing poisoning are standard vectors for fake customer care fraud. When users encounter a stuck payment, flight rescheduling issue, or misplaced courier, they often perform an ad-hoc search for 'Customer Care Toll Free Number' or look up local courier offices on online maps.

Cybercriminals create fraudulent sponsored search ads or edit public map location listings to replace the authentic phone number with their own mobile lines. When desperate consumers call, the scammer claims to be an authorized executive from the airline, bank, or payment gateway. Under the pretext of diagnosing the payment failure, the scammer instructs the caller to download remote assistance tools such as AnyDesk, TeamViewer QuickSupport, or RustDesk, or to scan a QR code to initiate a 'reverse test deposit'. Once remote access is established, the criminal monitors incoming bank OTPs and empties the victim's accounts.

Key defense principles:
1. Always obtain contact details strictly from inside your official mobile app or from the printed back of your debit card.
2. Search engine sponsored ad results and user-editable map entries frequently contain verified-looking fake phone numbers.
3. Legitimate customer support will never ask you to install AnyDesk or screen-mirroring apps on your smartphone.
